# Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics (summarised)

A broad checklist for audits; pair each finding with a more specific law from the other files when one fits.

1. **Visibility of system status** — always show what's happening (loading, saved, live, last updated). See Feedback, Doherty Threshold.
2. **Match with the real world** — users' words and concepts, natural order. See Mental Model, Curse of Knowledge.
3. **User control and freedom** — easy undo, back and exit, especially after mistakes.
4. **Consistency and standards** — same words and actions mean the same thing everywhere; follow platform conventions. See Jakob's Law, Similarity.
5. **Error prevention** — design out errors before writing error messages. See Constraints and Error Prevention, Postel's Law.
6. **Recognition rather than recall** — visible options and information. See Recognition over Recall, Working Memory.
7. **Flexibility and efficiency** — shortcuts and accelerators for frequent users without burdening new ones. See Progressive Disclosure.
8. **Aesthetic and minimalist design** — every extra element competes with the relevant ones. See Occam's Razor, Cognitive Load.
9. **Help users recognise, diagnose and recover from errors** — plain-language messages that say what went wrong and how to fix it.
10. **Help and documentation** — ideally unnecessary; when needed, searchable, task-focused and short. See Paradox of the Active User.
